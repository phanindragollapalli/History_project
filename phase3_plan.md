## 2. Central Working Thesis and Theoretical Framework

### 2.1 Central Thesis
> *Between the late nineteenth century and 1961, caste and religious relations in the upper Ravi basin of Chamba were shaped not primarily by static scriptural hierarchies, but by the material organisation of production and exchange operating across sharp ecological gradients. The altitudinal differences between alpine Brahmaur (~7,000 ft), terraced Chitrari (~5,800 ft), and forested Maingal (~4,000 ft) established distinct physiological ceilings and livelihood niches. These local economies generated specialized forms of mutual dependence between pastoralists, cultivators, artisans, and service groups. Crucially, expanding market commodities did not eradicate customary obligations: cash contracts, crop shares, unhusked grain perquisites, ritual meat entitlements, and commercial dairy marketing operated concurrently. Social hierarchy and inter-community co-existence were thus continuously reproduced, negotiated, and contested through locally specific economies constrained by mountain ecology and filtered through shifting colonial classification systems.*

### 2.2 Theoretical Integration: Historical Materialism and the Annales School
The analysis synthesizes two complementary paradigms through an evidentiary progression:
$$\text{Ecological Conditions} \longrightarrow \text{Available Productive Strategies} \longrightarrow \text{Organisation of Labour \& Resources} \longrightarrow \text{Forms of Exchange \& Dependence} \longrightarrow \text{Social Relations \& Status Negotiation}$$

1. **Marxist Method (Historical Materialism):**
   - *Control of Productive Resources:* Who owned or controlled arable land, transhumant flocks, buffalo herds, iron tools, and pasture permits?
   - *Labour Organization:* Who supplied labour and under what constraints? (Flock owner *malundis* vs. wage-shepherd *pohals*; high-caste landholders vs. attached *Hali* ploughmen).
   - *Forms of Compensation:* Co-existence of cash wages (Rs. 2/mo *pohal* wages), crop-shares (tenancy shares), customary grain perquisites (*khalian* threshing sheaves for *Lohars*), and ritual meat shares (*ghardi* sheep neck for *Sipis*).
   - *Status Contestation:* Contestation of untouchability and plough taboos through social mobility movements (attached *Halis* adopting *Aryas–Halis* identity in Chitrari).
2. **Annales Method (*Longue Durée* & Environmental History):**
   - *Physical Geography & Altitude Relief:* Altitudinal zonation (~7,000 ft alpine, ~5,800 ft terraced basin, ~4,000 ft forested slopes) and seasonal snowlines.
   - *Biological Ceilings on Livestock:* Cold-induced lactation drop in water buffaloes (collapsing from 9 to 4 seers/day) barring dairy transhumance from alpine Brahmaur and concentrating it in sub-Himalayan Maingal.
   - *Temporal Rhythms:* Rhythms of seasonal transhumance across the Dhaula Dhar and Pir Panjal passes.
   - *Material Technology:* The wooden plough (*hal*) with iron share, hand shearing scissors (*katar*), frame pit-loom (*rachh*), bamboo basket (*kilta*), combined dwelling-byre (*kotha*), and crop watchtower (*machan*).

#### 1.2 Resolve Known Comparability and Consistency Issues
1. **Rose Date:** Standardize citation to **1911–1919** (*Glossary of the Tribes and Castes*, Vol. I 1919, Vol. II 1911, Vol. III 1914; compiled from 1881/1891 censuses). Acknowledge that Rose is an ethnographic compilation, not a census.
2. **Relationship Count Scope:** Disaggregate the 12 relationships: **10 village-level relationships in 1961** (R1–R4 in Brahmaur, R5–R7 in Chitrari, R8–R10 in Maingal) and **2 regional/macro links** (R11: 1881 trans-Dhaula Dhar tenure; R12: regional Rehara silversmithing).
3. **Frequency Normalization Formula:** Recompute and verify normalized frequencies per 1,000 words:
   $$\text{Normalized Frequency} = \left(\frac{\text{Absolute Keyword Hits}}{\text{Total Searchable Words}}\right) \times 1,000$$
4. **Mention Counts vs. Population:** Explicitly distinguish textual prominence from numerical dominance (e.g., *Brahmin* mentioned 1.22/1k in Maingal despite being only 13.7% of population).
5. **Population Percentages:** Maintain clear distinctions between total village population, surveyed sample population, and household counts.
6. **Livestock Counts as Productive Strategies:** Treat herd size as an adaptation to terrain and vegetation, not a crude measure of per-capita wealth.
7. **Scale Separation:** Prevent conflation between British Punjab Province, Chamba Princely State, Wazarat/Tehsil, and Village levels.
8. **Categorical Drift Across Eras:** Avoid assuming identical meanings for categories between 1881 (paternal caste), 1931 (depression earners/dependents), and 1961 (village household monographs).
9. **OCR and Quotation Accuracy:** Verify all quotations against scanned PDF pages; preserve archaic and dialect spellings (*pattu*, *gardu*, *khalian*, *chola*, *dora*).
10. **Causal Claims vs. Observations:** Differentiate documented facts (e.g., 0 buffaloes counted, 1 failed trial) from broader inferences (regional biological laws).

### PART 4: Verification, Quality Control, and Final Handoff

#### 4.1 Twenty-Five Point Quality Control Checklist
- [ ] **1. Research Question:** The essay explicitly answers the approved research question.
- [ ] **2. Thesis:** The introduction establishes a contestable thesis sustained through the conclusion.
- [ ] **3. Systematic Comparison:** Brahmaur, Chitrari, and Maingal are compared across all analytical themes.
- [ ] **4. Marxist Theory:** Materialism, property, division of labour, and barter/cash coexistence are actively applied.
- [ ] **5. Annales Theory:** Altitude relief, biological limits, seasonal transhumance, and material culture are actively applied.
- [ ] **6. Archival Hierarchy:** Administrative levels (Province ➔ State ➔ Wazarat ➔ Village) are strictly distinguished.
- [ ] **7. Quantitative Accuracy:** All population, household, and livestock numbers match verified source tables.
- [ ] **8. Denominator Precision:** All percentages clearly state whether they refer to persons, households, or animal head.
- [ ] **9. Quotation Precision:** Verbatim quotations are faithful to original scans and line citations.
- [ ] **10. Frequency Metric:** Normalized frequencies are defined as textual prominence ($/1,000$ words), not population shares.
- [ ] **11. Network Traceability:** Every network edge is tied to a specific source passage and payment form.
- [ ] **12. Network Scope:** The 1961 village network is distinguished from regional/1881 historical links.
- [ ] **13. Crosswalk Rigor:** Categorical drift is analyzed; term disappearance is not equated with group extinction.
- [ ] **14. Thematic Coding:** Passage theme counts are interpreted as excerpt characteristics, not population prevalence.
- [ ] **15. Close Reading:** KWIC windows lead into full contextual close readings.
- [ ] **16. Source Criticism:** Winter census timing distortions (February census undercounting mobile Gaddis/Gujjars) are noted.
- [ ] **17. OCR Limitations:** The "absence fallacy" is addressed (absence in OCR $\neq$ historical absence).
- [ ] **18. Alternative Explanations:** Competing hypotheses (e.g., religious taboos on buffaloes vs. economic failure) are evaluated.
- [ ] **19. Analytical Sequence:** Every major finding follows: Observation ➔ Verification ➔ Comparison ➔ Interpretation ➔ Alternative Explanation ➔ Limitation.
- [ ] **20. Word Count:** Main essay text sits between 3,000 and 4,000 words (excluding frontmatter and references).
- [ ] **21. Figure Citations:** Figures 1, 2, and 3 have captions, source notes, and are discussed in text.
- [ ] **22. Table Citations:** Summary tables have clean booktabs formatting and clear units.
- [ ] **23. Clean LaTeX Code:** Compiles cleanly with standard packages without unescaped special characters.
- [ ] **24. Bibliography:** Formal citations for Ibbetson (1883), Rose (1911–1919), Khan (1933), and Singh (1961).
- [ ] **25. Conclusion:** Summarizes findings, answers the question, and notes future historical questions.

#### 4.2 Explicit Negative Guidelines ("What Not to Spend Time Doing")
- **Do NOT** add machine-learning or unnecessary NLP models just to make the project look complex.
- **Do NOT** generate excess charts that do not answer a specific historical question.
- **Do NOT** rehash raw OCR mechanics in the essay; summarize only what impacts historical interpretation.
- **Do NOT** treat word frequencies as direct proxies for population, wealth, or power.
- **Do NOT** claim caste vanished, markets fully replaced custom, or coexistence was frictionless without evidence.
- **Do NOT** generalize from three village monographs to the entire Western Himalayas without qualification.
- **Do NOT** use 1961 micro-data as if it directly describes conditions in 1881.
- **Do NOT** postpone citations and source checks until after drafting.
